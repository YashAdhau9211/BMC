## 2. Subject Introduction

Service Management is the set of specialized organizational capabilities for enabling value for customers in the form of **services**.[file:6]  
In IT and software companies, it provides a structured way to design, deliver, operate, and improve IT‑enabled services such as cloud platforms, banking apps, UPI payments, food delivery apps, telecom services, etc.[file:5]

Well‑designed service management helps organizations:
- Deliver consistent quality to customers.
- Balance **cost, risk, utility, and warranty**.[file:6]
- Coordinate roles and responsibilities using models like **RACI**.[file:5][file:12]
- Build strong **service relationships** between provider and consumer.[file:5]

Exam point: In most syllabi, Unit 1 is purely conceptual and theory‑based – perfect for scoring full marks if you can write structured, well‑explained answers.

- **Service:** A means of enabling value co‑creation by facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve, without the customer having to manage specific costs and risks.[file:5]

### 4.2 Concept: Service Management

#### Definition (exam‑style)

**Service Management** is the application of specialized organizational capabilities to deliver value to customers in the form of services, by effectively managing products, processes, people, and partners.[file:6]

- Aligns **IT services** with business needs (for example, ensuring UPI app supports bank’s digital strategy).[file:5]

### 4.3 Key Concepts in Service Management

The key concepts you must master in Unit 1 are:

1. **Value and value co‑creation.**
2. **Organizations, service providers, and service consumers.**
3. **Products and services.**
4. **Outputs and outcomes.**
5. **Costs and risks.**
6. **Utility and warranty.**[file:6][file:5]

#### 4.3.1 Value and value co‑creation

- **Value** is the perceived benefits, usefulness, and importance of something.
- In service management, value is **co‑created** through **active collaboration** between provider and consumer, not delivered one‑way.[file:5]

Example:
- UPI payment service has value only if:
  - Bank provides secure, always‑on infrastructure.
  - App provider offers a user‑friendly interface.
  - Customer uses it correctly and keeps PIN safe.

Exam tip: In answers, always mention that value is **subjective** and **co‑created**.

#### 4.3.2 Organizations, providers, consumers

- An **organization** is a person or group of people that has its own functions, responsibilities, authorities, and relationships in order to achieve its objectives.[file:5]
- The same organization can act as **service provider** in one relationship and **service consumer** in another.

Example:
- A fintech startup is a **provider** of a mobile wallet to retail customers.
- The same startup is a **consumer** of cloud services from AWS.

#### 4.3.3 Products and services

- A **product** is a configuration of resources (people, technology, information, processes) that is offered to consumers.[file:6]
- A **service** uses one or more products to enable **outcomes** for customers.

Table: Product vs service

| Aspect            | Product                                   | Service                                               |
|-------------------|-------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| Nature            | Tangible or packaged offering             | Intangible value experience                          |
| Ownership         | Often transferred to customer             | No ownership transfer; access/usage only             |
| Example (IT)      | Smartphone, laptop, router                | Mobile network plan, cloud hosting, streaming         |
| Role in ITIL      | Building block used by services           | Main focus of service management                      |

[file:6]

Industry example:
- Smartphone (product) + telecom network (service) + OTT app (service) together create end‑to‑end experience for user.

#### 4.3.4 Outputs and outcomes

- **Output:** Direct deliverable from an activity or service.[file:6]
- **Outcome:** Result that brings value to a stakeholder.[file:6]

Example – Food delivery app:
- Output: Order confirmation, tracking screen, delivered food.
- Outcome: Customer does not need to cook, saves time, feels satisfied.

Exam tip: Providers control **outputs**, customers care about **outcomes**. This line is very scoring.

#### 4.3.5 Costs and risks

- **Cost:** Amount spent by provider to create/deliver service, and by consumer to use it (fees, effort, time).[file:6]
- **Risk:** Uncertain event that can impact value – can be **negative (threat)** or **positive (opportunity)**.[file:3]

In services, provider usually takes on some risks that the consumer would otherwise have to manage.[file:6]

Example – Cloud vs on‑premise:
- On‑premise: Customer bears hardware failure, power outage, security risk.
- Cloud: Provider takes responsibility for infrastructure, redundancy, and base security; customer still handles application logic and data usage.

#### 4.3.6 Utility and warranty

These are very important in Unit 1.

- **Utility (fit for purpose):** Whether the service has the right features and capabilities to meet customer needs.[file:6]
- **Warranty (fit for use):** Whether the service is available, reliable, secure, and has enough capacity when needed.[file:6]

Combined effect:

```text
         +-----------------------+
         |   Useful Service?     |
         +-----------------------+
           ^                  ^
           |                  |
   Utility (What it does)   Warranty (How well it works)
```

Example – Cloud storage service:
- Utility: Upload/download files, sharing, versioning.
- Warranty: 99.99% availability, geo‑redundancy, encryption, support SLAs.

If **either utility or warranty is missing**, value reduces.

### 4.4 Service Management Aspects

The syllabus highlights five major aspects:[file:6]

1. Products and services.
2. Outputs and outcomes.
3. Cost and risk.
4. Utility and warranty.
5. Service relationships.[file:5]

We already discussed the first four; now we focus on **service relationships**.

#### 4.4.1 Service relationships – overview

A **service relationship** is a cooperation between service provider and consumer, combining three main elements:[file:5]

1. **Service offering.**
2. **Service provision.**
3. **Service consumption.**
4. **Service relationship management** (across lifecycle).

Diagram – Service relationship components

```text
+---------------------------+
|      Service Offering     |  (Value proposition, packages)
+---------------------------+
             |
             v
+---------------------------+
|     Service Provision     |  (Infrastructure, processes, support)
+---------------------------+
             |
             v
+---------------------------+
|    Service Consumption    |  (User activities, usage, outcomes)
+---------------------------+
             ^
             |
+---------------------------+
| Service Relationship Mgt  |  (Feedback, SLAs, improvement)
+---------------------------+
```

##### (a) Service offering

- Formal description of one or more services, designed to meet specific needs of a target consumer group.[file:5]
- Includes details of **utility, warranty, prices, and terms**.

Example: Jio fiber plan – 150 Mbps speed, unlimited data, OTT apps, 24x7 support.

##### (b) Service provision

- All activities performed by provider to deliver the service according to offering.[file:5]
- Includes infrastructure, networks, data centers, monitoring, support teams, change management, etc.

Example: Jio’s network operations center monitoring fiber, technicians maintaining lines, service desk handling complaints.

##### (c) Service consumption

- Activities performed by consumer to use the service – requesting access, using apps, paying bills, providing feedback.[file:5]

Example: User connects Wi‑Fi, streams Netflix, logs issues on app.

##### (d) Service relationship management

- Joint activities of provider and consumer to continuously improve value co‑creation – managing SLAs, feedback, incidents, and improvement initiatives.[file:5]

Example: Periodic customer satisfaction surveys, outage root cause analysis, plan upgrades.

Exam tip: For a 10‑mark question on service relationships, always structure answer around **offering, provision, consumption, and relationship management**.

### 4.5 Service Management Roles

Service management uses various roles to ensure clear accountability. Common roles (ITIL‑style) include:[file:5][file:8]

- **Service owner:** Accountable for end‑to‑end delivery and performance of a specific service.
- **Process owner / practice owner:** Responsible for effectiveness of a particular practice (for example, incident management owner).
- **Service desk agent:** First‑line contact for users; logs, classifies, and resolves incidents/requests.[file:8]
- **Incident manager:** Coordinates major incident resolution, ensures quick service restoration.
- **Change authority / Change Advisory Board (CAB):** Accountable for authorizing high‑risk changes.
- **Relationship manager:** Manages interactions with customers and key stakeholders.[file:8]
- **Technical support teams:** Provide specialized expertise for infrastructure, applications, and networks.

Role clarity is often documented using **RACI models**.

---

### 4.6 RACI Model for Role Mapping

#### Definition

A **RACI matrix** is a responsibility assignment chart that maps activities or deliverables to four role types: Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed.[file:5][file:12]

- **R – Responsible:** Person(s) who perform the work.
- **A – Accountable:** Person who owns the outcome and takes final decisions; only one per task.
- **C – Consulted:** People who must be consulted before or during work.
- **I – Informed:** People who must be kept updated on progress and results.

#### ASCII diagram – RACI matrix

```text
Activities vs Roles

                 Service   Service   Incident   Change
                 Owner     Desk      Manager    CAB
-------------------------------------------------------
Log incident        I        R          C        I
Resolve incident    A        R          R        I
Approve change      A        I          C        R
Notify customer     R        R          C        I
```

[file:5][file:12]

#### Why RACI is important in service management

- Avoids **confusion** about who should do what.
- Reduces **blame games** during incidents and outages.
- Ensures that **governance** and **communication** are clearly defined.[file:12]

Real outage example:
- In global IT outages (for example, CrowdStrike, AWS DNS failures), clear RACI mapping helps quickly identify:
  - Who is accountable for incident declaration.
  - Which team is responsible for fix.
  - Who must be consulted (security, vendors).
  - Who must be informed (business, customers, regulators).[file:12]

#### Model Answer

**Introduction**  
Modern organizations depend on IT‑enabled services such as online banking, e‑commerce, UPI payments, and cloud platforms. Managing these services systematically is the focus of service management.[file:5][file:6]

**Definitions**  
- A **service** is a means of enabling value co‑creation by facilitating outcomes that customers want to achieve, without the customer having to manage specific costs and risks.[file:5]
- **Service management** is a set of specialized organizational capabilities for enabling value for customers in the form of services.[file:6]

**Value and value co‑creation**  
- **Value** is the perceived benefits, usefulness, and importance of a service.[file:6]
- In service management, value is **co‑created** through active collaboration between provider and consumer, not delivered one‑way.  
  - Provider offers reliable service, support, and improvements.
  - Consumer uses the service correctly, provides feedback, and follows security guidelines.[file:5]

### Q4. Explain the concepts of cost and risk in service management. How does a service provider help a customer manage costs and risks?

#### Answer Outline

1. Introduction.
2. Definition of cost.
3. Definition of risk.
4. Types/examples of costs and risks for provider and consumer.
5. How provider reduces or transfers risks.
6. Real‑world example (cloud / SaaS / data center).
7. Conclusion.

#### Model Answer

**Introduction**  
Every service involves costs and risks for both provider and consumer. Service management must balance value with fair cost and acceptable risk.[file:6]

**Definitions**  
- **Cost:** The amount of money, effort, and other resources spent by an organization to create, deliver, and consume a service.[file:6]
- **Risk:** A possible event that could cause harm, loss, or missed opportunity, or make it harder to achieve objectives.[file:3]

**Costs in services**  
- **Provider costs:**
  - Infrastructure (servers, data centers, networks).
  - Licenses, tools, cloud subscriptions.
  - People (salaries, training, support teams).
  - Continuous improvement and compliance costs.[file:3]
- **Consumer costs:**
  - Subscription fees, one‑time setup fees.
  - Internal effort to adopt the service.
  - Training, data migration, support time.

**Risks in services**  
- **Technical risks:** Outages, performance degradation, security breaches.[file:3][file:12]
- **Business risks:** Loss of revenue, reputational damage, regulatory penalties.
- **Operational risks:** Single points of failure, skill gaps, poor change management.[file:3]

**How service providers help manage costs and risks**  
- **Economies of scale:** Provider invests once in infrastructure and spreads cost across many consumers.
- **Standardization:** Standard services reduce complexity and control risk.
- **Expertise and practices:** Use of ITIL practices – incident management, risk management, change enablement, service continuity – reduces probability and impact of incidents.[file:8]
- **Risk transfer:** Customers transfer some risks (for example, hardware failure, DDoS attacks) to provider through contractual SLAs and warranties.[file:3][file:12]

**Real‑world example – Cloud migration**  
- Organization moves ERP system to multi‑cloud environment using several suppliers.
- Risks: Data loss, downtime, IAM gaps, supplier failure, cost overruns.[file:3]
- Providers manage these through redundancy, security controls, SLAs, and integrated incident processes.

**Conclusion**  
Cost and risk are central aspects of service management. Good providers design services to optimize total cost of ownership and reduce or share risks so that customers can focus on their core business.

**Exam keywords:** cost, risk, risk transfer, SLAs, economies of scale, cloud migration.

### Q5. Define utility and warranty. Explain how both dimensions together determine the value of a service.

#### Answer Outline

1. Introduction.
2. Definition of utility.
3. Definition of warranty.
4. Diagram – utility vs warranty.
5. Examples (cloud storage, food delivery, telecom).
6. Link to value co‑creation.
7. Conclusion.

#### Model Answer

**Introduction**  
In ITIL‑based service management, value is primarily determined by two aspects of a service: **utility** and **warranty**.[file:6]

**Utility – definition**  
**Utility** is the functionality offered by a product or service to meet a particular need; it is often called **"fit for purpose"**.[file:6]

**Warranty – definition**  
**Warranty** is the assurance that a product or service will meet agreed requirements such as availability, capacity, continuity, and security; it is often called **"fit for use"**.[file:6]

**Diagram – utility and warranty**

```text
             VALUE OF SERVICE

       High Value  +---------------------+
                   |  High Utility       |
                   |  High Warranty      |
                   +---------------------+
       Medium      |  High Utility,      |
                   |  Low Warranty  -->  Crashes, slow
                   +---------------------+
                   |  Low Utility,       |
                   |  High Warranty  --> Reliable but useless
                   +---------------------+
       Low Value   |  Low Utility,       |
                   |  Low Warranty       |
                   +---------------------+
```

**Examples**  
1. **Cloud storage service**
   - Utility: Upload/download files, share with others, versioning.
   - Warranty: 99.99% uptime, data redundancy, encryption, 24x7 support.

2. **Food delivery app**
   - Utility: Search restaurants, place orders, track delivery.
   - Warranty: Deliver within 30–40 minutes, low failure rate, refund policy.

3. **Telecom data plan**
   - Utility: Internet access, calls, SMS.
   - Warranty: Network coverage, minimum speed, call drop rate limits.

**Link to value co‑creation**  
- If utility is strong but warranty is weak, users may get required features but face poor experience (for example, feature‑rich app that crashes often).
- If warranty is strong but utility is weak, users get a reliable service that does not meet their real needs.
- Maximum value is achieved when **both utility and warranty are optimized** for target customers.[file:6]

**Conclusion**  
Utility ensures the service does the right things, and warranty ensures it does them consistently and reliably. Together, they determine perceived value in service management.

**Exam keywords:** fit for purpose, fit for use, availability, capacity, continuity, security, value.

### Q8. Explain the RACI model. How is it used in service management to map roles and responsibilities? Provide a sample RACI chart.

#### Answer Outline

1. Introduction.
2. Full form of RACI.
3. Definition of each letter.
4. Steps to create RACI.
5. Sample RACI table for incident/change.
6. Benefits.
7. Conclusion.

#### Model Answer

**Introduction**  
In complex IT services, many teams are involved. The RACI model helps clearly define who does what for each activity, avoiding confusion and delays.[file:5][file:12]

**Full form and meaning**  
- **R – Responsible:** Person(s) who perform the actual work.
- **A – Accountable:** Person who owns the result and makes final decisions; exactly one per activity.
- **C – Consulted:** People who must be consulted before or during the activity.
- **I – Informed:** People who must be kept updated about progress and results.

**Steps to create RACI**  
1. List major activities or deliverables in rows.
2. List key roles in columns.
3. For each cell, assign R, A, C, or I.
4. Ensure each activity has one Accountable person.

**Sample RACI chart – Major incident**

```text
Activity / Role         Service   Incident  Network   Security  Business
                         Owner    Manager   Team      Team      Owner
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Detect incident            I         R        R         C         I
Declare major incident     A         R        C         C         I
Investigate root cause     C         R        R         R         I
Apply fix                  C         A        R         C         I
Communicate to customers   A         C        I         C         R
Close incident             A         R        C         C         I
```

[file:5][file:12]

**Use in service management**  
- Applied to **incident management, change enablement, problem management, deployment, and release** activities.[file:8]
- During large outages, RACI clarifies who is accountable for decisions and communications, preventing delays.

**Benefits**  
- Eliminates role confusion.
- Supports compliance and governance.
- Helps design **standard operating procedures (SOPs)** and **runbooks**.

**Conclusion**  
The RACI model is a simple but powerful tool in service management to map and communicate responsibilities, especially in multi‑team and multi‑supplier environments.

**Exam keywords:** RACI, Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed, responsibility assignment matrix.
